Most promising development sites

Every parcel gets a score out of 100. Points come from mixed-use or apartment zoning, being in a transit station area, room to add density compared with nearby approvals, lot size, recent applications nearby and a Secondary Plan. Points come off for TRCA, ravine, natural heritage and heritage constraints.

Left out: condos, designated heritage, floodplain land, parks, employment and institutional zones, and small lots. Sites with development applications on them are flagged; those with a recent rezoning, site plan, condo or subdivision file are hidden unless you include them. This is a screening score from open data. It does not know ownership, leases or whether an owner wants to sell.

Assumptions
Starting values are rough Toronto high-rise condo figures for late 2026. Sale prices are set by market area, a grouping of the City's wards, and each site uses the price for the area it is in. The area prices are estimates anchored to the 2026 citywide average for new condos (about $1,189/sf), so replace them with your own comparables. Changes are saved in this browser.
Residual land value = revenue − hard and soft costs − development charges − demolition − sales − financing − profit, then divided by (1 + land closing % + parkland % + CBC % + land carry). This is a screening estimate, not an appraisal: it ignores HST, phasing, inclusionary zoning and site-specific costs.
Data: Development Applications, Toronto Centreline and City Wards, City of Toronto Open Data (Open Government Licence – Toronto). Site analysis uses Property Boundaries, Zoning By-law 569-2013 (zones, height and coverage overlays), Secondary Plans, Site and Area Specific Policies, Major Transit Station Areas, the Heritage Register, Ravine Protection and 3D Massing, all City of Toronto Open Data. This page holds a snapshot of those datasets.
